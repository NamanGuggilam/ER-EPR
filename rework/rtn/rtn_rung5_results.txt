  WHY THAT tau IS NOT 'REAL CONTENT' -- decomposition:
    (a) inversion: tau(S, L_RT) = -0.203, vs the spec's tau(1/S, L_RT) = +0.227.
        RT says L_RT is proportional to S, but d_boundary = 1/S, so a
        dictionary that HOLDS predicts anti-monotone distances.  The
        sign of tau is set by the 1/S convention, not by the physics.
        (The two are not exact negatives because d_boundary is the
        draw-average of 1/S, and mean(1/S) need not rank exactly
        opposite to mean(S) -- a few near-tied interior pairs swap.)
    (b) matched-geometry control: tau(S, chain min_cut) = +0.734
        discordant pairs vs min_cut: 0 of 48 strictly ordered
        -> the tensor entropies obey RT on the network's OWN geometry
           (zero discordant = every interior interval out-entangles
           every end interval).  tau-b sits below 1 only because
           min_cut has two tied values; the discordant count is the
           clean test.
    (c) compare (b) with (a): the SAME entropies give tau = +0.734 against
        the chain's own min cut and -0.203 against the circle's RT
        length.  Only the comparator changed, so that gap is the
        TOPOLOGY MISMATCH: an open-chain entropy compared to a circle's
        RT length.  Pair (1,6) alone is maximally far on one side and
        adjacent on the other.  That is an index mismatch -- the class
        of error this rebuild began by removing -- not content.

[7] VERDICT
  The boundary side is now a genuinely independent quantum-information
  computation, and it does obey RT -- on its own open-chain geometry
  (control: zero discordant pairs in every run).  But the spec pairs
  it with the wrong comparator: a CIRCLE's RT length, through a 1/S
  convention that inverts the dictionary.  The low Kendall tau comes
  from that inversion plus the chain-vs-circle mismatch, not from
  independent structure; and the bulk H1 is circulant-forced.  Fixing
  the comparator (open-chain bulk, or a periodic MPS on a ring) and the
  convention (compare S to L_RT, or 1/S to 1/L_RT) is the next step.
